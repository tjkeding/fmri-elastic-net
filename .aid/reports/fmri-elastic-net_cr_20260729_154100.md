    <finding id="F3" severity="major" category="robustness">
      <location file="fmri-elastic-net.py" lines="565-575" />
      <description>_hotelling_t2 computes df2 = n - p_dim (n_folds - n_moderator_contrasts) without guarding against df2 <= 0. The F-distribution transformation and CDF are undefined for non-positive df2. Additionally, np.linalg.inv(S) fails on a singular covariance matrix when n_folds < p_dim.</description>
      <evidence>Line 573: df2 = n - p_dim. Line 574: F = (n - p_dim) / (p_dim * (n - 1)) * T2. Line 575: p_value = float(1.0 - f_dist.cdf(F, df1, df2)). For K=6 nominal moderator with 5-fold CV: df2 = 5 - 5 = 0 (division by zero). For K > n_folds + 1: df2 < 0 and S is rank-deficient.</evidence>
      <literature>Standard Hotelling T-squared to F transformation requires df2 = n - p > 0 (Hotelling, 1931; Anderson, 2003, An Introduction to Multivariate Statistical Analysis, Ch. 5).</literature>
      <impact>Pipeline crash (LinAlgError from singular covariance, or ValueError/NaN from invalid df2) for any design where n_folds <= K-1. Example: 6-level nominal moderator with 5-fold CV.</impact>
      <recommendation>Add a guard returning NaN p-value with a warning when df2 <= 0. Add a rank check on S before inversion for the near-singular case.</recommendation>

    <finding id="F4" severity="major" category="inferential_calibration">
      <location file="fmri-elastic-net.py" lines="1955" />
      <description>All _write_tier1_report calls apply ttest_1samp across K fold-level coefficients, treating them as independent. In K-fold CV, each pair of folds shares (K-2)/(K-1) of training data (89% for 10-fold), inducing positive between-fold covariance. The naive variance estimator is downward biased, producing anti-conservative p-values.</description>
      <evidence>Line 1955: t_stat, p_val = ttest_1samp(coef_matrix, popmean=0, axis=0). This treats K fold-level coefficient vectors as i.i.d. observations. The dependence applies to all Tier 1 calls: main-effect, interaction, and Hotelling omnibus.</evidence>
      <literature>Bengio and Grandvalet (2004, JMLR 5:1089-1105): no universal unbiased estimator of K-fold CV variance exists; naive estimator is downward biased. Bates, Hastie, and Tibshirani (2024, JASA 119(546)): standard CV confidence intervals have coverage below nominal. Bayle, Bayle, Janson, and Mackey (2020, NeurIPS 33): corrected asymptotic-variance estimators developed specifically because naive treatment is invalid. All three sources: consensus. Note: BH-FDR correction does not address this; FDR controls multiplicity but requires valid (uniformly distributed under the null) input p-values, which the biased t-test does not provide.</literature>
      <impact>Type I error exceeds nominal alpha for all Tier 1 p-values. Magnitude is algorithm- and distribution-dependent, not a fixed correction factor. The two-tier complementary sensitivity design partially mitigates: Tier 1 is positioned as a liberal screen (sensitivity-prioritized), and anti-conservative p-values are directionally consistent with this role. Tier 2 bootstrap CIs provide the independent confirmatory gate.</impact>
      <recommendation>Document as a known limitation (Option A, user-approved). Tier 1 p-values carry downward bias of unknown magnitude due to fold dependence and should not be interpreted as exact. Tier 2 CIs are the confirmatory inference. A corrected variance estimator (Nadeau and Bengio 2003, or Bayle et al. 2020) would be a reasonable future enhancement but warrants its own brainstorm session. This is a pre-existing architectural property, not interaction-specific.</recommendation>
    </finding>
